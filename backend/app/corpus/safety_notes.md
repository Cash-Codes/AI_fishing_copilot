# Sea Fishing Safety — Essential Guidance

## Check the Forecast Before You Go

Weather at sea changes faster than on land. A force 3 breeze onshore can be force 5 offshore, and a forecast for 1-metre waves can quickly become 2-metre waves on exposed marks.

Always check:
- Wind speed and direction from the Met Office marine forecast (gov.uk/weather)
- Wave height from Windguru, Magic Seaweed, or the Coastguard NCI forecast
- Tide times and range for your specific harbour from Admiralty EasyTide or a current almanac
- Any harbour or coastguard notices for the area

The Met Office Inshore Waters forecast is the most authoritative source for UK coastal waters. Check it the evening before and again on the morning of the session.

## Never Fish Alone at Night

Night fishing produces excellent Cod, Conger, and Bass results, but shore fishing alone in darkness on rocky ground or harbour walls is high-risk. If you slip, lose footing, or are taken by a wave, there is no one to raise the alarm.

Rules for safe night fishing:
- Always fish with at least one companion
- Tell a responsible person (family member, friend) exactly where you are going and what time you expect to return
- Agree a check-in time — if you miss it, they should call the Coastguard (999 in an emergency, or VHF Channel 16)
- Carry a mobile phone in a waterproof case or bag
- Wear a head torch with fresh batteries and carry a spare

## Tidal Cut-offs

A tidal cut-off occurs when the rising tide blocks the route back to safety. It is the most common cause of serious fishing incidents on rocky shore marks.

How to avoid a cut-off:
- Study the tide table before leaving the car; identify the time of high water
- Walk your intended route at LOW water and assess which sections will be covered as the tide rises
- Begin your exit at least one hour before the relevant section of path or rock floods
- Never assume you can outrun the tide — rocks become slippery extremely quickly as water touches them
- If you are cut off, stay high, call 999 for Coastguard, and stay visible. Do NOT attempt to wade through tidal water over rocky ground

The tide can rise by 1 metre in 30 minutes on a spring tide on many UK coasts. It is always faster than it looks when you are watching from high ground.

## Life Jackets and Buoyancy Aids

A life jacket should be worn whenever fishing from a boat, kayak, or stand-up paddleboard. The RNLI and RYA recommend automatic inflate (150N) life jackets as the minimum standard for offshore use.

For shore fishing, a life jacket is less common but strongly recommended when:
- Fishing alone on exposed harbour walls or rocky headlands
- Night fishing in autumn and winter when seas are unpredictable
- Fishing in areas with known fast-rising tidal currents

A buoyancy aid (50N) provides swimming assistance but will not turn an unconscious person face-up. For coastal sea fishing an automatic inflate life jacket offers considerably more protection.

## Weather Windows and Conditions Thresholds

Practical guidance for different conditions:

- Wind below 15 knots: generally fishable from harbour walls and sheltered marks for all skill levels
- Wind 15–25 knots: suitable for experienced anglers on sheltered marks only; avoid exposed headlands
- Wind above 25 knots: most shore marks become unsafe; some very sheltered harbour walls remain fishable
- Swell above 2 metres: any mark with tidal surge or wave wash becomes hazardous
- Swell above 3 metres: abandon all coastal headland and rocky shore fishing

After storms, even when the storm has passed:
- Swell can persist for 24–48 hours after the wind has dropped (residual swell)
- Rocky surfaces remain exceptionally slippery for hours after being wet
- Floating debris in the water can mask tidal currents and obstacles

## Emergency Contacts

- UK Coastguard: 999 (emergency) or 02392 552100 (non-emergency)
- VHF Channel 16: universal distress channel; monitored by all vessels and coastguard
- RNLI: the RNLI operates lifeboat stations around the UK coastline; call 999 to activate

If you see someone in difficulty in the water, call 999 immediately and throw any available buoyancy aid (life ring, fender, even a sealed plastic bottle) towards them. Do not enter the water yourself unless you are a trained lifesaver — the majority of drowning deaths in coastal incidents involve would-be rescuers.

## Equipment Checklist

Before any sea fishing trip carry:
- Tide table for your location (printed or downloaded, not relying solely on phone signal)
- Mobile phone in a waterproof container, fully charged
- First aid kit including plasters, wound closure strips, and antiseptic
- Knife for cutting line (not for use as a weapon; carry it safely)
- Forceps or pliers for unhooking fish and removing embedded hooks
- Warm clothing and waterproof jacket even in summer — sea temperatures remain cold and wind chill is significant
- Water and food for sessions over 3 hours
- Head torch (night sessions) with spare batteries
