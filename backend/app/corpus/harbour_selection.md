# Choosing the Right Harbour for UK Sea Fishing

## Proximity vs Quality

The nearest harbour is not always the best choice, but it is often the most practical. Travel time to a fishing mark has real cost: arriving tired, in deteriorating weather, or after the optimal tidal window has passed can ruin a session. For beginners especially, starting close to home is the right approach.

The ideal balance:
- Within 30–45 minutes travel for a session lasting 4–6 hours
- Far enough to encounter genuinely good fishing (major population centres rarely have exceptional inshore marks immediately adjacent)
- Accessible at the state of tide you plan to fish

## Understanding Harbour Descriptions

Fishing marks are described in terms of target species, technique, and tidal access. Key phrases:

- "Year-round" — species available in all seasons but may not be at peak
- "Charter port" — boats available for hire; often signals quality offshore marks nearby
- "Estuary" — usually calmer conditions, Flounder and Bass marks, but tidal access matters
- "Rocky ground" — typically Wrasse, Pollack, and Conger habitat; requires heavier tackle
- "Exposed" — subject to wind and swell; only fishable in settled conditions
- "Sheltered" — calm in most weather; may sacrifice specimen fish for reliability

## Shelter and Safety

Exposed headlands and open beaches produce the best fishing in settled conditions but become dangerous quickly when weather deteriorates. Harbours with breakwaters or natural shelter can often be fished safely when nearby open coasts are inaccessible.

Criteria for a sheltered harbour:
- Breakwater providing protection from prevailing SW to W wind
- Harbour walls where you can turn your back to the weather
- Easy exit routes to car parks and roads (not cliff paths)
- Mobile phone signal, or within sight of the coastguard station

Always identify an exit route before you start fishing. A harbourside mark accessible at low water may be cut off when the tide rises.

## Charter Fishing vs Shore Fishing

Charter boats give access to marks beyond shore range: offshore wrecks, deep reefs, and isolated sandbanks that produce larger specimens. The trade-off is cost (typically £50–90 per person per day) and availability.

Charter fishing suits:
- Targeting offshore species: large Pollack, Bream, Tope, Shark
- Deep-water Cod and Ling on wreck marks
- When inshore shore fishing is producing poor results

Shore fishing suits:
- Budget-conscious anglers or beginners developing technique
- Species that regularly approach the shoreline: Bass, Mackerel, Flounder, Codling
- Locations where charter boats are unavailable or booked in advance

## Tidal Access and Timing

Many harbour marks are only accessible at certain states of the tide. Common constraints:

- Rocky reef marks: fishable at low water, covered (and unsafe to access) at high
- Estuary channels: fish move in on flood; the best position changes through the tide
- Pier ends: sometimes only passable on foot at certain tidal states due to gates or steps
- Harbours with drying areas: only accessible by boat at certain tidal heights

Check your planned mark at both the tide you intend to fish and the state the tide will be when you need to leave. Many anglers get into difficulty because they arrive at low water and don't account for the flood cutting off the return route.

## Seasonal Harbour Choice

Some harbours fish better at certain times of year:

- Winter (Nov–Mar): sheltered, south-facing harbours for Cod and Whiting; avoid exposed north-facing marks in prevailing north-west gales
- Spring (Apr–May): estuary mouths as Bass arrive inshore; Bass staking out the flood-tide marks
- Summer (Jun–Sep): anywhere with offshore charter access for Bream and Tope; pier fishing for Mackerel
- Autumn (Sep–Nov): the most productive all-round season; Bass peak, Cod arriving, Mackerel still present; most harbours productive

## Distance and the Recommendation Score

Our scoring system balances harbour distance against species match. A harbour 10 km away that is well-known for your target species in season will often outscore a 2 km harbour that has no track record for that fish. Understanding this trade-off helps interpret the confidence score you receive.

The "closest" preference setting maximises the distance component of the score; "best-chance" maximises the species and season match. Use "closest" when you have limited time or transport; "best-chance" when you want the most productive session possible.
