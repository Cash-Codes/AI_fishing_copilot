# UK Sea Fish Species Activity Patterns

## Bass (Dicentrarchus labrax)

Bass are the most prized inshore species in the UK. They are warm-water fish: activity increases significantly once sea temperatures climb above 12°C, typically from May in the south-west and June further north.

Best conditions for Bass:
- Flood tide over shallow rocky ground, beaches, and estuary mouths
- Dawn and dusk in clear summer conditions; overcast days allow all-day feeding
- Water temperatures between 14–20°C
- Wave action that creates white-water surf over sandy beaches
- Strong tidal flow around headlands and through estuary channels

Bass move inshore from April and are best from May through October in the south-west, with peak fishing often in September when fish have been feeding all summer. They migrate offshore as water temperature drops below 10°C in November.

## Cod (Gadus morhua)

Cod are a classic cold-water target, moving inshore on UK east and north coasts from October through March. Numbers have declined significantly from historic highs, but quality Codling are still taken from Yorkshire, Northumberland, Scottish east coast, and some south-coast locations.

Best conditions for Cod:
- Cold, coloured water following rough weather — stirs up food on the seabed
- Night tides over spring neaps produce the most consistent catches
- Large baits: whole squid, peeler crab, cocktail baits
- Clean sandy or rough ground from harbours, piers, and beaches
- Slack or slow tidal periods allow fish to feed without fighting current

Offshore Cod are available year-round from northern wreck marks but true inshore sport is winter-only.

## Mackerel (Scomber scombrus)

Mackerel arrive in inshore UK waters from June and depart in September or October. They are shoaling pelagic fish that travel in huge numbers near the surface during summer months. Mackerel are often the first species a beginner catches and are excellent eating.

Best conditions for Mackerel:
- Clear water and surface temperatures above 15°C
- Look for working seabirds — terns diving indicate a Mackerel shoal below
- Simple feather traces or spinners worked fast near the surface
- Most active in morning and evening, but will feed all day in good conditions
- Common from piers, harbour walls, and charter boats from June to September

Mackerel are also essential as fresh bait for Tope, Conger, Shark, and large Pollack.

## Pollack (Pollachius pollachius)

Pollack are one of the best-fighting reef and wreck species in UK waters. They are present year-round on deep rocky ground but move closer inshore and to shallower reefs from April to October when water temperature rises.

Best conditions for Pollack:
- Broken ground, kelp edges, and wreck marks in 10–40 metres
- Low-light periods: early morning and late evening are most productive
- Slack water or weak current — Pollack ambush prey rather than chasing it in strong current
- Slowly worked soft-plastic lures and natural sandeel on a flying collar

Larger specimens (over 4 kg) are almost exclusively found on deep offshore wrecks. Shore-caught Pollack to 2 kg are common from rocky headlands and cliff marks on the flood tide.

## Flounder (Platichthys flesus)

Flounder are a beginner-friendly species that tolerate very low salinity, making them the dominant flatfish in estuaries. They are present year-round in estuaries but are most actively targeted from October to April.

Best conditions for Flounder:
- Estuarine mud and sand in 1–4 metres of water
- Flood and ebb channels; fish the gully edges
- Ragworm is the most reliable bait, with live marine worms outperforming frozen
- Often found very close to the bank — casting only 10–20 metres can be enough
- Cold water improves catches; summer Flounder are smaller and less numerous

## Sea Bream (Black Bream, Spondyliosoma cantharus)

Black Bream arrive on south coast reefs and wrecks from May, driven inshore by warming water to spawn. They provide excellent sport on light tackle and are found in tight shoals.

Best conditions for Bream:
- Rocky reef and wreck in 10–25 metres; respond poorly to sandy ground
- Small hooks (size 1–2) and light traces — Bream have small, fussy mouths
- Slow drifts produce better results than anchoring in most situations
- Warm water (above 14°C) is essential; April marks the earliest arrival
- Peak catches: June–August from south coast charter marks

## Wrasse (Labrus bergylta)

Ballan Wrasse are the most common large Wrasse in UK waters, reaching 4 kg on productive south and west coast marks. They are extremely colourful, strong fighters over rocky ground, and are exclusively a warm-water target.

Best conditions for Wrasse:
- Dense kelp and rocky ground in 2–10 metres depth
- Warm, settled conditions; Wrasse become lethargic in cold water below 10°C
- Live or fresh crab, prawn, and ragworm close to rocky structures
- Active from May to October; peak sport July and August
- Light float or LRF (Light Rock Fishing) tactics with small lures

## Tope (Galeorhinus galeus)

Tope are the UK's most accessible shark species, growing to 25 kg. They arrive on sandy offshore grounds from May and are almost exclusively caught from boats.

Best conditions for Tope:
- Sandy or mixed ground offshore in 15–40 metres
- Whole fresh Mackerel or Herring on a running trace
- Last two hours of the flood tide concentrates fish in sandy gullies
- May to August peak; some marks fish into September
- Strong tackle essential: 30–50 lb class rods with 80 lb mono trace

## Conger Eel (Conger conger)

Conger Eels are the heaviest fish caught by UK shore and boat anglers, with specimens exceeding 50 kg from deep wrecks. They are year-round but most active in summer.

Best conditions for Conger:
- Rocky ground, harbour walls, and wreck marks after dark
- Strong baits: whole Mackerel, large squid, presented on heavy wire trace
- Last of the ebb and first of the flood — Conger follow bait fish on the tide
- Summer nights produce the best shore Conger from Cornwall and south Wales
- Boat fishing produces the largest specimens from deep water wrecks

## Whiting (Merlangius merlangus)

Whiting are a common winter catch from piers and beaches, often accompanying Cod in cold-water conditions. They are a useful sport fish and excellent bait for Tope and Conger.

Best conditions for Whiting:
- Sandy and mixed seabed in 5–20 metres
- October to February; peak December–January
- Lugworm and ragworm on size 1/0–2/0 hooks
- Often caught alongside Codling on night tides over spring neaps
